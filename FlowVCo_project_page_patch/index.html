Our model creates colorful and temporally consistent video colorizations while following the versatility of reference-frame color guidance.

Abstract

Video colorization aims to generate semantically appropriate and temporally consistent colors for grayscale videos. Existing diffusion-based approaches often face challenges in high training resources or poor cross-frame consistency due to their reliance on stochastic denoising processes. To address these limitations, this paper proposes an optical flow-guided diffusion framework that explicitly utilizes inter-frame motion to enhance temporal coherence. We explicitly inject optical flow into the network during training and introduce an optical flow-guided cross-frame attention mechanism, which leverages precise optical flow correspondences to modulate attention between frames during sampling. This ensures stable and artifact-free color propagation under complex motion patterns. Moreover, our framework incorporates reference images as additional guidance to improve controllability and color fidelity. To reduce dependence on large-scale real-world video data, we design a hybrid data synthesis pipeline that first pre-trains the model using synthesized optical flow-image pairs and then fine-tunes it on a limited set of high-quality videos generated by modern video synthesis models. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance in terms of training efficiency, temporal consistency, color realism, and user controllability compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches.

Method

We explicitly model inter-frame correspondence with optical flow, while retaining the generative prior of an image diffusion model.

Optical-Flow Conditioning

Forward and backward optical flows are injected as explicit motion conditions together with the grayscale input and reference-frame latent, with only minimal additional trainable parameters.

OFCFA

Optical Flow-guided Cross-Frame Attention uses flow correspondence to constrain cross-frame attention, improving identity preservation and reducing content drift, ghosting, and color flicker.

Hybrid Training

We first pre-train with synthesized optical-flow/image pairs and then fine-tune on a limited set of high-quality videos generated by modern video synthesis models.

Qualitative Results

Our method maintains stable object colors across motion and better follows the given reference than competing colorization methods.

Additional results from the supplementary material show improved semantic color assignment and cross-frame consistency on diverse scenes.

Ablation & Efficiency

The ablation study shows steady gains as OFCFA, the reference frame, and forward/backward optical flows are introduced. The full model reaches CDC 3.303, PSNR 26.35, SSIM 0.950, and LPIPS 0.207.

The supplementary material reports a runtime of 3.56 s/frame on DAVIS. The method is slower than CNN-based BiSTNet, but substantially faster than the compared diffusion-based L-CAD and I2VEdit methods.

Failure Cases

Rapid motion can make optical flow blurry and increase occlusion, weakening optical-flow guidance and OFCFA when a frame is far from the reference.

In complex scenes, an incomplete prompt can also lead to incorrect color assignments. The paper points to richer semantic guidance as a promising direction for future work.
